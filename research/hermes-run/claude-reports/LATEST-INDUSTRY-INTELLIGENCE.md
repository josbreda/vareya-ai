## 1. EU customs and small-parcel regulation

- **The EUR150 duty-exemption threshold for goods imported from outside the EU was removed effective 1 July 2026** (S-16, Dutch Customs / Belastingdienst-Douane, confirmed live). This applies specifically to goods entering the EU from non-EU countries (e.g., a customer in an EU country ordering from a non-EU webshop) — it does **not** apply to shipments moving between two EU member states, which is Vareya's core fulfilment business. This distinction matters directly for C-0xx claims about "customs clearance" capability: if Vareya's own claims register asserts customs-handling capability, that capability is only relevant for the subset of Vareya clients shipping from outside the EU into it, not for standard intra-EU order fulfilment.
- **A EUR3-per-item handling charge for low-value imports (under roughly EUR150) is being phased in as part of the same reform**, described as a temporary measure from 1 July 2026 to 1 July 2028 pending the EU Customs Data Hub's full rollout (S-17, European Commission DG TAXUD). The exact regulation number and the "26 March 2026 political agreement" date cited on the Commission's own overview page could not be independently cross-checked against EUR-Lex directly in this pass — the primary legislative text is JavaScript-rendered and not fetchable with the tools available this session (**B-05**). Treat the EUR3 figure and the 2028 sunset date as **corroborated but not independently verified against primary legal text**.
- **The EU Customs Data Hub itself is a multi-year phased rollout (cited phase dates: 2028, 2031, 2034 for different trader categories)** — again sourced from the Commission's own overview, not independently verified against a primary legal instrument (same B-05 limitation).
- **No 2026 changes were found to the IOSS (Import One-Stop Shop) VAT regime** itself, which has operated under its 1 July 2021 rules with no update found in this pass (S-18). This is a negative finding under a partial-quota limitation (**B-04**) — absence of evidence found is not the same as confirmed absence of any change; recommend a follow-up check when WebSearch quota is available.
- **Bottom line for Vareya's claims:** any claim implying Vareya "handles EU customs complexity" should be scoped precisely — the 2026 reform is about non-EU-origin imports, not intra-EU shipping, which is Vareya's actual core service. An unscoped "we handle the new customs rules" claim risks implying relevance to a problem most of Vareya's actual client base (EU-to-EU shippers) does not have.

## 3. SEO / AEO / AI-findability — current state (checked 2026-09-01)

- **Google discontinued FAQPage rich results in Search as of 7 May 2026** (S-21, S-19). Vareya's FAQPage structured data (present on multiple pages) is not harmful but produces no remaining Google-specific visual benefit; it should be described internally as legacy schema, not an active SEO lever.
- **Google's own guidance is that no special "AI Overviews optimization" exists beyond normal high-quality indexing** (S-22) — any internal claim that specific content changes were made "for AI Overviews" as a distinct, provable channel should be treated cautiously; Google does not document a separate optimization surface for it.
- **IndexNow benefits Bing, Naver, Seznam, and Yandex/Yep — not Google** (S-23). If Vareya's SEO tooling submits via IndexNow, this should be described accurately as a non-Google-specific action.
- **llms.txt is an unofficial community proposal (Answer.AI / Jeremy Howard), not an adopted standard by any major search or AI provider** (S-29). It is not documented as a ranking or citation factor by OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, or Perplexity in any of their current crawler/bot documentation (S-25, S-26, S-27, S-28). Vareya's llms.txt file is real and well-formed but should continue to be described as a low-cost convenience file, not a proven visibility mechanism — and its factual country-count error (see `CLAIMS-RISKS.md` Risk 4) should be fixed regardless, since AI crawlers that do read it would ingest the wrong number as ground truth.

## 4. Fulfilment competitor market

Researched via official company sites, official press releases, and (where an official page was bot-blocked) Wayback Machine snapshots explicitly dated as such. Third-party/aggregator sources are labeled "external perception" and never blended into factual claims. All checks 2026-09-01 unless a snapshot date is given. WebSearch quota was exhausted partway through this research (**B-04**), so several items rely on WebFetch and search-snippet text rather than a directly rendered page — flagged individually below where that applies.

### Closest direct competitors to Vareya's segment (500+ orders/month, EU-based, category-aware)

- **byrd (getbyrd.com)** — 12 fulfilment centers across Austria, Germany, France, UK, and (new as of June 2026) Poland. Explicitly segments customers as "Startups: 500-1,000 shipments/month," "Established Retailers: 1,000-25,000/month," "Enterprises: 25,000+/month" (S-37) — this is the single closest published customer-segmentation match to Vareya's own 500+/month target found in this research. Its new Poland facility (announced 11 June 2026, quoting co-founder Petra Dobrocka) is explicitly marketed as cutting fulfilment costs "15 to 25 percent" (S-37, primary/official source). byrd has no stated Benelux facility and no stated category specialization in cosmetics/wellness — both plausible points of differentiation for Vareya.
- **Monta (gomonta.com)** — Netherlands-headquartered (Bleskensgraaf), 20+ warehouses across NL/DE/UK/FR, 3,000+ webshops served, named clients including AkzoNobel and Pink Gellac (S-38). Broadest integration stack found in this research, including Dutch-specific channels (bol.com) that byrd/ShipBob/Huboo do not emphasize. Likely Vareya's most direct same-country generalist competitor; differentiator for Vareya is category depth (cosmetics/skincare/supplements) against Monta's 25-year, multi-vertical generalist positioning.
- **ShipBob** — has one actual Netherlands fulfilment center (Venray, opened June 2023) plus UK sites; global scale (50-60+ centers, 1 billion+ units fulfilled as of 5 January 2026, per ShipBob's own press) (S-39). Markets aggressively on technology/AI (an "Anthropic-verified fulfillment connector" launched 4 August 2026) and a new delivery-date guarantee beta ("ShipBob Promise," 12 May 2026, US-parcel-only so far). Its EU footprint is thin relative to its global scale and marketed primarily as a US-first platform with EU as an add-on.

### Structural market change found — not previously flagged in any internal Vareya document reviewed

**Active Ants has been absorbed into "Paxon,"** a new pan-European 3PL brand formed by **Bnode** (formerly bpostgroup) consolidating four previously separate logistics companies — Active Ants, Base Logistics, Radial, and Staci — into a single entity spanning at least 9 countries (UK, NL, BE, DE, PL, IT, ES, FR, plus China/North America) (S-40). No dedicated official rebrand-announcement press release with an exact date was located; the Paxon site's sitemap was generated 8 April 2026, and an independent Bnode press item dated 24 June 2026 confirms Paxon was fully operational by that date. **If any current Vareya competitive-positioning material still treats "Active Ants" as a standalone mid-market Dutch competitor, that is now outdated** — it is part of a considerably larger group.

### Other market participants checked

- **Huboo** — went through an actual insolvency and pre-pack administration rescue in December 2024 (failed payroll for 600 staff), backed by BlackRock-managed funds, Ada Ventures, and Atalla Capital; subsequently acquired Manchester-based Sorted Group Holdings for GBP1 in April 2026, creating a combined entity claiming 100M+ parcels/year (S-41, trade press — BusinessCloud/Retail Technology Review, not Huboo's own statements). This is a real, material stability event for a similarly-sized competitor and is worth noting as a talking point: financial stability/solvency is a legitimate, evidence-backed differentiator argument for a smaller, demonstrably solvent operator. Huboo's own site blocks automated fetching (403/Cloudflare); pricing/integration details could only be sourced from third-party directories (fulfill.com), not confirmed directly — labeled accordingly.
- **Bleckmann** — fashion/apparel-specialized (garment-on-hanger, repacking), not a direct competitor to Vareya's cosmetics/wellness/supplements niche. Low relevance.
- **Salesupply** — self-describes as a "4PL" bundling fulfilment with outsourced customer service and returns management, a broader service scope than pure pick-pack-ship. Worth noting as a category of competitor (bundled-services) rather than a direct one-to-one comparison.
- **Hive (hive.app)** — Berlin-based, claims "500+ brands, EUR1B+ in sales, 100M+ items shipped" and markets aggressively on ROI percentages (cost/revenue claims), tightly integrated with Shopify/Klaviyo/Gorgias — a horizontal DTC-focused platform with no stated category specialization.
- **DHL Supply Chain (DHL Fulfillment Network)** — official Shopify partnership announced 26 May 2025, expanding through 2025-2026; a search-snippet-sourced (not directly page-confirmed) signal suggests a volume floor around "3,000 orders/month" (S-42) — if accurate, this sits well above Vareya's 500+/month target segment, suggesting DHL Supply Chain is not chasing the same segment head-on today, though it could compete for Vareya's better-performing clients as they scale past Vareya's sweet spot. DHL's own pages timed out repeatedly in this research and this figure could not be independently re-confirmed on DHL's own site — treat as a signal, not a verified fact.
- **Amazon FBA / Multi-Channel Fulfillment (MCF)** — a live threat for the overlap segment (brands already selling on Amazon who could use the same inventory pool to fulfil Shopify orders), but a dedicated official MCF page could not be located/fetched in this research (candidates 404'd; Seller Central documentation is login-gated) — this is a research gap, not a confirmed absence of the service.
