## Post 1 — The volume number that hides the real question

Two webshops can ship exactly the same number of orders a month and still need completely different fulfilment quotations.

Total order volume alone is a starting point, not a profile. What matters underneath it: where those orders actually go (a country-by-country split, not "worldwide"), what the packed parcel looks like — not the product, the packed parcel — and how many items and order lines the average order actually contains.

A quotation built on volume alone is closer to a guess. A quotation built on the full operational picture can hold up.

We've written up the nine inputs a 3PL genuinely needs before it can quote properly, and two anonymised examples of what a strong versus incomplete profile looks like in practice.

## Post 2 — Product dimensions are not parcel dimensions

A common quotation mistake: giving a 3PL the dimensions of the product, not the packed parcel.

Carriers price what actually leaves the warehouse — boxed, padded, labelled — and that is often meaningfully bigger and heavier than the product alone. Get this wrong at the quotation stage and the number you were quoted stops meaning much once real orders start shipping.

Suitable smaller parcels have combined dimensions below 900 mm and a maximum length of 600 mm. Whatever the size range, the rule is the same: quote the packed parcel, not the product.

## Post 3 — SKU count is not the same as order complexity

A catalogue of 1,000 SKUs where most orders contain a single item is a different operation from a catalogue of 100 SKUs where the average order contains six.

Total SKU count mostly affects storage and pick-location complexity — how much of the warehouse your catalogue occupies. Items and order lines per order more directly affect how many pick actions a warehouse team completes per shipment.

A high SKU count does not automatically mean more items in every order. Worth separating the two before you ask a 3PL for a quotation — it changes what they're actually pricing.

## Post 4 — Same stock count, completely different pallet count

An anonymised comparison worth keeping in mind: a pallet of office chairs might hold a handful of units. A pallet of something like nasal strips could hold thousands.

Same stock count, wildly different physical footprint. If you're estimating your storage need for a fulfilment quotation, "units" alone won't get you there — product type and packaging matter just as much.

If storage requirements are genuinely unknown at this stage, that's a valid, honest answer. Better than a guess dressed up as a figure.

## Post 5 — What a strong quotation profile still leaves open

We looked at an anonymised example: an apparel brand, ~150 SKUs, ~1.3 items per order, clear platform, clear key markets — a genuinely strong starting profile.

Even so, several things were still missing before a final quotation could be prepared: destination percentages, packed parcel dimensions rather than product dimensions, return volume, and storage detail.

The lesson isn't "try harder." It's that a detailed profile earns you a meaningful first assessment — final quotations still need a short list of specifics filled in afterwards. Knowing that going in saves a round of back-and-forth later.
