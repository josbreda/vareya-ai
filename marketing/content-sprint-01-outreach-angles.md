# Content Sprint 01 — Outreach Angles
**Article:** What information does a 3PL need to prepare a fulfilment quotation?
**Status:** READY FOR JOS REVIEW — do not send without approval
**Principle (unchanged from `marketing/scan-first-outreach.md`):** scan-first, no meeting request, no fake urgency, no savings promises
**Source of truth:** `content/claims-register.md` v1.2; article draft `content/review/what-information-does-a-3pl-need-for-a-fulfilment-quotation.md`

These are angles for using the article as an outreach asset, not full outreach drafts. Each angle lists who it fits, the opening hook, the article link, and what NOT to say — matching the risk areas already flagged in the claims report.

---

## Angle 1 — "You've been asked for a quote and don't know where to start"

**Fits:** leads early in the process — platform + rough volume known, nothing else worked out yet. Closest match: CASE-INCOMPLETE-001 profile.

**Hook:** frame the article as preparation, not a pitch. "Before you request quotations from anyone, here's what they'll actually need from you" reads as useful regardless of who they end up choosing.

**Link:** article, not the scan directly — let the reader self-select into the scan after reading.

**Do not say:** anything implying their current information is inadequate or that they're "doing it wrong" — CASE-INCOMPLETE-001 is illustrative, not a mirror held up to the prospect.

---

## Angle 2 — "You have a solid profile, but the quote still feels vague"

**Fits:** leads who have already provided a reasonably detailed profile (platform, SKU count, rough volume, key markets) but are getting quotes that still feel like guesses. Closest match: CASE-STRONG-PROFILE-001 profile.

**Hook:** the "Questions to ask when comparing 3PL quotations" section — genuinely useful even if they don't choose Vareya, which is the point of a scan-first, non-pushy angle.

**Link:** article section on comparison questions; secondary CTA to `/request-fulfilment-quote/` rather than the scan, since this audience has likely already got some way through a quotation process elsewhere.

**Do not say:** that other quotations they've received are wrong or dishonest — frame it as "questions worth asking anyone," not a competitor jab.

---

## Angle 3 — "Packed parcel, not product" as a specific, useful correction

**Fits:** brands with a physical, packaging-heavy product (apparel with tags/polybags, cosmetics with secondary packaging, anything padded or boxed) where the gap between product size and packed size is likely large.

**Hook:** a narrow, concrete correction rather than a general pitch — "most quotation requests we see use product dimensions, not packed parcel dimensions, and that gap is usually where the first requote comes from."

**Link:** "Packed parcel dimensions and weights" section directly.

**Do not say:** the blocked 2 kg comparative claim (RB-1) or anything implying Vareya has the cheapest or sharpest rates in this weight range — stick to the mechanism (carriers price the packed parcel), not a rate claim.

---

## Angle 4 — "SKU count won't tell a 3PL what your picking actually looks like"

**Fits:** brands with a wide catalogue relative to order size (many SKUs, low items-per-order) — a segment likely to have been quoted on SKU count alone by other providers and be surprised by pick-and-pack costs.

**Hook:** the SKU-vs-items-vs-order-lines distinction as a genuinely differentiated insight — most competitor pages don't separate these (confirmed via `SOURCE-REGISTER.md`'s competitor gap analysis).

**Link:** "SKU count versus items and order lines" section.

**Do not say:** any comparison to how competitors calculate this — the article and this outreach angle explain Vareya's own framing, not a claim about how others get it wrong.

---

## Angle 5 — Storage-density angle for bulky or ultra-light SKUs

**Fits:** brands at either extreme — large/bulky products (furniture-adjacent, homeware) or very light/small products (beauty minis, accessories) where "unit count" alone misleads on storage need.

**Hook:** the office-chairs-vs-nasal-strips contrast, reused directly from the article — concrete, memorable, not sales-heavy.

**Link:** "Storage requirements" section.

**Do not say:** any specific pallet capacity, warehouse size or throughput figure — these remain excluded per the register (no capacity figures).

---

## Cross-angle rules (apply to all five)

- No meeting request in the first message — link to the article or the scan, let the reader choose the next step.
- No fake urgency ("limited spots," "this month only," etc.) — none of the source claims support this and it is excluded by tone rules regardless.
- No savings or price claims — CLAIM_ALL_IN describes the rate structure (fixed, all-in), never a specific number or a "cheaper than" comparison.
- Volume mention, if used, must reproduce CLAIM_VOLUME verbatim ("Vareya is generally best suited to brands shipping 500 or more orders per month") — never paraphrased into "we love high-volume brands" or similar.
- None of RB-1–RB-4 may appear in any outreach copy built from these angles.
