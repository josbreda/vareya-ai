## 1. Raymond founder post — READY (niet gepubliceerd)

**Target audience:** DTC-founders met grensoverschrijdende verkoop, LinkedIn-bereik van Raymond.
**Hook:** "Two webshops can ship the same number of orders a month and still need completely different fulfilment quotations."

*Two webshops can ship exactly the same number of orders a month and still need completely different fulfilment quotations. Total order volume is a starting point, not a profile. What matters underneath: where orders actually go — a country split, not "worldwide" — what the packed parcel looks like, and how many items and order lines an average order contains. A quotation built on volume alone is close to a guess. One built on the full operational picture can hold up. We wrote down the nine inputs a 3PL genuinely needs before quoting properly — plus two anonymised examples of a strong versus incomplete profile.*

## 2. Jos operational post — READY (niet gepubliceerd)

**Target audience:** Operations/ecommerce-managers, praktijkgericht.
**Hook:** "A catalogue of 1,000 SKUs where most orders contain one item is a different operation from a catalogue of 100 SKUs where the average order contains six."

*A catalogue of 1,000 SKUs where most orders contain a single item is a different operation from one with 100 SKUs where the average order contains six. SKU count mostly affects storage and pick-location complexity; items and order lines per order drive the pick actions per shipment. A high SKU count does not automatically mean more items in every order. Separate the two before asking a 3PL for a quotation — it changes what they're actually pricing. The full breakdown, with a quotation-readiness checklist:*

## 3. Vareya company post — READY (niet gepubliceerd)

**Target audience:** Bedrijfspagina-volgers; algemene awareness.
**Hook:** "Product dimensions are not parcel dimensions."

*Product dimensions are not parcel dimensions. Carriers price what actually leaves the warehouse — boxed, padded, labelled — and that is usually bigger and heavier than the product alone. Get this wrong at quotation stage and the quoted number stops meaning much once real orders ship. New on our Knowledgebank: what information a 3PL needs before it can prepare a meaningful quotation — the nine inputs, common mistakes and a checklist.*
